The tumbleweed cycle on the mesa

Much of Rio Rancho was platted long before anyone built on it, so a lot of parcels, especially out in Rio Rancho Estates, are still sand, chamisa and whatever weeds the last rain brought up. Russian thistle loves that ground, dries out by fall and lets go in the wind.

Those plants then collect wherever something stops them: a fence, a wall, the side of a parked trailer, or the next lot over. A vacant parcel next to built homes can become the neighborhood's tumbleweed catcher, which is often what brings a complaint.

The City of Rio Rancho can require owners to keep lots clear of weeds and litter, and it runs its own code enforcement separate from Albuquerque. If you've received a notice, the deadline on it sets our schedule.

What decides the size of a Rio Rancho cleanup

No two lots out here take the same effort. These are the things we look at on the walk.

Road access
Lots on unpaved roads at the edge of the city may limit what size truck and trailer can get in, especially after rain or in deep sand.
Years since the last cleanup
A lot cut last fall is light work. One left for a decade can have chamisa stands and buried tumbleweed layers.
Fence and wall drifts
Tumbleweeds packed against a neighbor's wall take hand work to pull out without damaging the wall.
Dumped trash
Raw lots attract dumping. Mixed debris has to be sorted from the brush before it's hauled.
Cut or clear to dirt
Mowing weeds down is lighter work than removing chamisa and roots, and it leaves the sand less likely to blow.

Cleaning up a lot you've never stood on

Plenty of our Rio Rancho customers bought their lot from out of state or inherited it. We can find the parcel, walk it on our own and go over what we saw with you by phone, with photos.

Rio Rancho lot cleanup questions

My lot is on a dirt road out past the pavement. Can you still get to it?

Usually. We check the route on the walk-through and pick a truck and trailer that can handle the sand. If the road is washed out after a storm, we may need to wait for it to dry.

Do I need the survey pins found just to cut weeds?

Not to cut weeds in the middle of the lot. If the job runs up to a line you're unsure of, or you're clearing brush along a neighbor's fence, finding the corners first avoids arguments later.

Can you get the lot ready for listing photos?

Yes. We can cut it down, pull the tumbleweeds off the fences and remove trash so buyers can see the ground and the view.

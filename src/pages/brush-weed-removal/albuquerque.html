Why the weeds look different in each part of town

On the Westside and Northwest Mesa, loose sand off Coors gets disturbed and Russian thistle moves in fast. By fall those plants dry, snap off at the base and roll until a block wall stops them, which is why some backyards fill to the top of the fence.

Up in the Northeast Heights and Foothills, the problem is more dry grass and brush growing between rock and decomposed granite. Slopes and boulders there mean a lot of the cutting is done by hand with trimmers and brush cutters rather than a machine.

In the North and South Valley, river silt grows tall kochia and weeds wherever water collects. Older infill lots near Downtown, Nob Hill and the university tend to collect as much litter and dumped debris as weeds.

Brush & weed removal questions in Albuquerque

Will you clear the alley strip behind my house?

Yes, if you want it included. Alleys and the ground outside a back wall are where tumbleweeds collect, so tell us when you call and check your notice to see whether that area is listed.

Can you cut the weeds on my Westside lot without turning it into blowing sand?

Usually, yes. Cutting weeds low instead of scraping the lot to bare dirt leaves the roots holding the sand. Scraping makes sense when you're about to build, not for routine cleanup.

Can we set up a second visit after the monsoon?

Yes. A lot of Albuquerque owners book one cleanup after the summer rains and another before the windy months. We'll remind you when it's time if you want.

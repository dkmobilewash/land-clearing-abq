Wind, sand and gravel yards on the West Mesa

Most yards in Paradise Hills are xeriscaped with gravel or left as native sand. Weeds root easily in both, and in a gravel yard they're hard to pull without dragging the rock along with them.

The wind that crosses the mesa carries tumbleweeds and loose trash until they hit a block wall. Side yards, back corners and the strip outside a rear wall fill up first, and those are the spots neighbors notice.

Homes on the edge of the neighborhood, near open mesa ground and the golf course, tend to collect the most. Clearing once after the summer rains and again before the spring wind can keep a Paradise Hills yard from getting to the point where a neighbor writes in.

Complaints in the Special Zoning District

In the unincorporated part of Paradise Hills, the Paradise Hills Special Zoning Commission responds to written neighbor complaints, and weeds and trash are among the most common. Addresses inside Albuquerque city limits fall under the city instead. Either way, we can do the cleanup and send photos, but we can't tell you how the commission or the city will respond.

Paradise Hills cleanup questions

Can you get weeds out of my gravel without stripping the rock?

Yes. We pull or cut weeds in gravel by hand instead of scraping, so the rock stays where it is. If the gravel is thin or mixed with sand, we'll tell you before we start.

Do you take trash and old yard waste along with the weeds?

Yes, loose trash and yard waste go out with the weeds. Bulky items like old furniture or appliances can be added to the quote if you tell us about them.

How do I find out if my address is under the zoning commission or the city?

Bernalillo County can confirm which applies to your parcel. We can help you look it up during the estimate, but the county has the final word.

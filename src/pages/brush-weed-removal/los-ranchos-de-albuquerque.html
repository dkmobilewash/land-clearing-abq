What changes the work on a Los Ranchos property

Two neighboring properties can be very different jobs. Here's what moves the effort up or down.

How big and how tall
A one-acre field cut every year is quick. Several acres left alone for seasons takes longer and produces more to haul.
Woody growth mixed in
Elm and Russian olive sprouts need heavier cutting than grass and weeds.
Irrigation timing
Valley silt and loam stay soft after water. Machine work waits for firm ground.
Gate width
Narrow gates can limit us to smaller equipment or more hand work.
Haul or leave
Hauling everything off is more work than leaving cut grass on the field.

Planning around irrigation days

Many Los Ranchos fields are watered through MRGCD-managed acequias on a schedule. We like to cut a few days after the field dries and before the next water day, so the ground is firm and the grass you want to keep gets water soon after.

If you tell us your schedule when you call, we'll fit the visit to it.

Brush & weed removal questions in Los Ranchos de Albuquerque

Can you clear an old field so I can start irrigating it again?

Yes. We mow the weeds down and clear out woody sprouts so water can spread. If the field has humps and low spots, grading may also help and we can quote it separately.

Do I need to check with the village before brushing my fence line?

Cutting weeds and small sprouts is routine upkeep. Before taking out large trees along a fence, ask the Village of Los Ranchos whether any tree rules apply to your property.

Can cut grass and weeds stay on the field?

Yes, if you prefer. We usually recommend hauling anything that has gone to seed so it doesn't replant the field.

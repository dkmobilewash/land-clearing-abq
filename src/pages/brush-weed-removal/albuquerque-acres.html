Thinning a lot you plan to build on later

Many owners in North Albuquerque Acres hold a vacant parcel for a while before they build. In the meantime, weeds and tumbleweeds move in and neighbors with finished homes start to notice.

A cleanup now doesn't have to mean clearing the whole lot. We cut weeds, remove dead shrubs and pull out tumbleweeds, and leave the native plants so the lot still looks like the Northeast Heights, not a dirt pad.

  • Arroyo channels and drainage paths
  • Boulders and rock outcrops to keep

Rock, slope and arroyos change how brush comes off

The ground rises toward the mountains, and many lots are a mix of decomposed granite, loose rock and caliche. Equipment can work the flatter, open parts of a lot, but rocky slopes and boulder piles usually get trimmers and hand crews.

Where an arroyo crosses the lot, we cut weeds out of the channel without reshaping it. Changing how water runs through an arroyo is a grading question for your engineer and the city or county, not part of a weed job.

Cut, don't scrape, on large lots

Scraping an acre to bare ground leaves it open to wind and runoff, and clearing larger areas may need a fugitive dust control permit from the local air quality program. For routine weed control, we cut growth low and leave the roots and native cover holding the soil.

Brush & weed removal questions in Albuquerque Acres

Can you clear my vacant lot while I'm living out of state?

Yes. We walk it on our own, send photos and a written quote, and send after photos when the work is done.

Will a brush cleanup now save work when I build?

Some. Clearing weeds and dead brush means less to remove later, but the pad and driveway area will still be cleared to the builder's plan when construction starts.

Does my HOA need to approve a weed cleanup?

Routine weed cutting usually doesn't. If your subdivision has architectural guidelines about native vegetation and you plan to remove a lot of it, check those guidelines first.

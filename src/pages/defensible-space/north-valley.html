Where valley lots meet the bosque

Valley properties closest to the Rio Grande often share a fence with the bosque, and even lots farther in have tree rows that run like fingers toward the house. Siberian elm drops dead branches, Russian olive and salt cedar grow into dense thickets, and the weeds under them dry out by late summer.

Add generations of farm life, like old pens, woodpiles and junk stacked behind a shed, and many North Valley lots carry more fuel than their owners realize.

Common fuels on valley lots

  • Dead Siberian elm limbs on the ground and in the canopy
  • Russian olive and salt cedar thickets
  • Dry weeds in unused horse pens
  • Old orchard prunings left in piles
  • Woodpiles and junk against outbuildings

How we work a fuel job on a valley lot

  1. 1

    Walk and mark

    We walk the lot with you, mark what stays and note wells, septic lids and buried irrigation you know about.

  2. 2

    Clear around the house

    Dead material and dry shrubs near walls come out first, and limbs over the roof get cut back.

  3. 3

    Open the tree rows

    We remove invasive thickets and dead wood, and limb the trees you're keeping up off the ground.

  4. 4

    Haul and finish

    Loads go out as we work, so there are no brush piles left sitting on the lot.

Leave the ditch out of it

Many North Valley lots are served by MRGCD ditches. We don't stack cut brush in a ditch or on its easement, and work on the ditch itself needs the district's approval.

What sets the size of a North Valley fuel job

These are the things that make one valley property more work than the next.

How dense the invasive growth is
A few Russian olives are a quick job. A solid wall of salt cedar takes much longer to cut and load.
Dead wood on the ground
Years of fallen elm limbs and orchard prunings add to the haul-off.
Soil moisture
Silt and clay that stay soft after irrigation may push us to hand work or a later date.
Access to the back of the lot
Narrow side gates and fenced pens decide what equipment can reach the tree rows.
Mixed debris
Junk piles and old lumber have to be sorted from brush before hauling.

Defensible space questions in North Valley

Am I required to have defensible space on my North Valley property?

That depends on your jurisdiction, and the North Valley is split between the city, the county and Los Ranchos. Check with your local fire department, or a Firewise program near you, for recommendations that fit your property. We don't decide what's required.

Can you thin an old orchard instead of taking it out?

Yes. We clear grass and dead wood from the orchard floor, remove dead trees and limb the live ones up, so the orchard stays but carries less fuel.

What do you do if the ground near the river is too wet for equipment?

We switch to hand crews where we can and bring machines back once it's firm. Rutted silt is slow to recover, so we'd rather wait.

Your back fence is where our work stops

The bosque behind many Corrales properties is public land, and we only thin on your side of the line. How much space to keep around buildings depends on the property, so follow guidance from New Mexico fire and forestry agencies, the village fire department or a Firewise program in your area. Thinning lowers the fuel; it can't guarantee what a fire will do.

Thinning without losing the cottonwoods

Defensible space in Corrales is rarely about cutting down big trees. The cottonwoods are part of the place, and taking out the invasive growth under and around them often does more good than removing them.

We limb cottonwoods up off the ground, clear the dead wood and leaf litter that piles beneath them, and remove the Russian olive and salt cedar that act as ladder fuel. Large limbs near power lines are for utility crews, not us.

Ground near the bosque and irrigated pasture can be soft, so we schedule machines for firm conditions and do more by hand when it's wet.

Corrales defensible space questions

Can you clear around my barn and hay storage?

Yes. We clear weeds, brush and dead wood from around barns, sheds and hay storage, and limb trees that hang over them.

Will you cut down my cottonwoods to lower the fire risk?

Only if you ask and the tree has a real problem. Usually we keep them and remove what's under and around them instead.

Can I burn the brush you cut instead of having it hauled?

Open burning is controlled by local rules and fire restrictions, so check with the village and fire department first. We haul or chip everything, so you don't need to burn.

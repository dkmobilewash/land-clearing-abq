Two fire edges inside one city

In the Northeast Heights and Foothills, many backyards end where open space at the base of the Sandias begins. Native brush and grass run right up to the back wall, and on windy days fire on a slope can move uphill quickly.

Along the river in the North Valley and South Valley, the edge is the bosque: cottonwoods, dead wood on the ground and thickets of salt cedar and Russian olive. Homes there face a different fuel, but the idea is the same.

In both places, we work on your property. Open space and the bosque are public land managed by others, and we don't cut there.

Start at the walls of the house

Fire and forestry agencies generally describe defensible space in zones, with the area right around the house kept leanest. That's where we start, because small things there, like a dead shrub under a window or a stack of firewood against a wall, matter most.

Recommended distances vary with slope and vegetation, so follow guidance from New Mexico fire and forestry agencies, your local fire department or a Firewise program in your neighborhood. We're glad to work from a plan they give you.

Near-house items we check

  • Firewood and lumber stacked against the house
  • Dead or dry shrubs under windows and eaves
  • Juniper and other shrubs pressed against walls
  • Branches hanging over the roof
  • Brush around a propane tank
  • Leaves and debris under decks

Defensible space questions in Albuquerque

My Foothills house backs onto open space. Can you clear past my wall?

No. Land beyond your property line is managed by others, and we only cut on your property. If you're worried about brush on the other side, contact the agency that manages it.

Can you follow a plan from the fire department?

Yes. If the fire department or a neighborhood Firewise program has walked your property and given you recommendations, bring them to the estimate and we'll quote that work.

Will you leave the cut brush as mulch?

Not close to the house, where a deep mulch layer can be fuel itself. We usually haul it all off, or chip it for use well away from the home if you want to keep it.

Grading on Los Ranchos fields and horse property

Re-leveling an irrigated field

Years of flood irrigation, gophers and weeds leave ridges and low corners. We level along the direction the water travels so it reaches the far end instead of pooling halfway.

Rutted paddocks

We knock down ridges, fill hoof holes with compacted material, and slope the paddock to shed water.

A new arena on an old pasture

After clearing, we cut and fill an arena base to an even, slightly sloped grade and compact it.

A wet yard beside the house

Valley silt and loam hold water when graded flat. We give the yard a fall away from the walls.

Working irrigated valley ground

The village sits on irrigated silt and loam. It's workable soil, but it gets heavy when wet and smears under tracks, so we schedule around your irrigation turn.

Field leveling here is less about a flat surface and more about a steady fall from where water comes in to where you want it to end. Tell us how you irrigate and we'll grade around it.

Village rules and mature trees

Los Ranchos has its own zoning and permits, separate from Albuquerque, so check with the village before larger projects or new structures. If a grading plan would remove large trees or cut close to them, ask the village whether tree rules apply to your property first. Ditches and their easements are usually MRGCD's, and work in or on them needs the district's approval.

Grading & leveling questions in Los Ranchos de Albuquerque

Should I grade before or after clearing the Russian olive along my field?

After. Clearing comes first so roots and debris are out of the way, then we level the strip where the tree row stood. We can quote both as one job.

Can you level a field without filling the ditch beside it?

Yes. We stay off the ditch and its easement. Any change to the ditch itself needs MRGCD's approval.

When is a good time of year for field leveling in the village?

Whenever the ground is firm and you're between irrigation turns. Right after a heavy watering or a monsoon storm, the silt ruts, so we wait.

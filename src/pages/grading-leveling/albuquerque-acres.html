Grading a raw Acres lot for a build

  1. 1

    Flag what stays

    We walk the lot with you and mark native plants, rock outcrops and view lines you want to keep, so clearing and grading stay inside the build area.

  2. 2

    Rough grade to the plan

    After a New Mexico 811 locate, we work from your builder's or engineer's plans and staking, cutting the uphill side and filling the downhill side. Rock and caliche get ripped or hammered where they're in the way.

  3. 3

    Build and compact the pad

    Fill goes in lifts, each compacted before the next, up to the pad elevation on the plan.

  4. 4

    Shape the runoff path

    Swales and slopes route storm water coming from uphill around the house instead of toward it.

  5. 5

    Finish grade

    A last pass smooths the ground around the house for landscaping and leaves the rest of the lot as natural as the plan allows.

Why two neighboring Acres lots can grade very differently

We look at all of these before we quote.

Slope toward the mountains
The steeper the lot, the more cut and fill it takes to set a level pad.
Rock and caliche
Decomposed granite moves easily; boulders and caliche layers need a ripper or hydraulic hammer.
Arroyos and flood zones
An arroyo or a mapped flood area can limit where the pad goes and how the lot is shaped.
Keeping native ground
Protecting existing vegetation means working in a tighter footprint, which takes more care.
How far back the house sits
A pad set deep on the lot means more ground to shape between it and the street.

Check flood maps and subdivision rules early

Some lots here have an arroyo or a portion mapped in a flood zone, and grading near an arroyo may need extra approvals. Rules also differ by parcel, city or county, and some subdivisions have architectural guidelines of their own. Larger grading may need a fugitive dust control permit. Settle these before the site plan is final; we're glad to build from your engineer's grading plan.

Regrading an established home in the Acres

Not every job here is a new build. Some homes were graded years ago in a way that now sends water toward a patio, garage or foundation, usually on the uphill side of the house.

For those, we trace where water comes onto the lot from above and where it needs to leave, then reshape the slope and add swales so it travels around the house. Working around mature landscaping and the views you bought the lot for is part of the job.

If a neighbor's lot sits below yours, we grade so the fix doesn't simply hand your water to them.

Grading & leveling questions in Albuquerque Acres

Who sets the pad elevation on my lot, you or the engineer?

It should come from your builder's or engineer's plans. We build the pad to that elevation and compact it in lifts, working from their plans and staking rather than guessing.

How do you keep grading from wiping out the native brush I want to keep?

We flag what stays before any machine arrives, keep equipment on a set path, and limit clearing to the areas the plan needs.

Part of my lot is in a mapped flood zone. Can it still be graded?

Often parts of it can, but grading in or near those areas may need extra approvals from the city, county or flood control authority. Check that first, and we'll work within whatever plan is approved.

Grading horse property on the village's two kinds of ground

On the east side of the village, close to the river and irrigated fields, the ground is soft and holds moisture. It shapes well but ruts under heavy equipment if we work it too soon after irrigation or rain, so we time the job for firm ground.

Heading west toward Rio Rancho, the soil gets sandier. Sand drains faster but won't keep a crisp edge, so arena bases and paddock slopes there need extra compaction to stay where we put them.

What Corrales owners usually ask us to grade

An arena that holds puddles

We regrade the base with a slight fall so water leaves the arena, then compact it before footing goes on. The footing is your choice; we build the shape underneath it.

Paddocks churned into ridges

Hooves and wet ground leave holes and humps. We cut down the high spots, fill the low ones, and slope the pen so it dries after a storm.

A pasture that floods unevenly

High ground stays dry while the low corner stands in water. Leveling to a steady fall helps irrigation water spread across the whole field.

Room for a trailer to turn

We level and compact a pad or loop near the barn so a truck and trailer can turn without cutting up the pasture.

Leave the ditch to the district

Ditches in Corrales are usually run by the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District, not by the property owner beside them. Grading in or beside a ditch or its easement typically needs MRGCD permission, and we won't reshape a ditch bank without it. Larger projects may also need something from the Village of Corrales, so ask the village early.

Questions about grading in Corrales

How soon after irrigating can you grade my pasture?

It depends on your soil and how much water went on. Near the bosque we usually need the ground to firm up first, so share your irrigation schedule and we'll plan around it.

Will grading an arena hurt the cottonwoods next to it?

We mark root zones during the walk-through and keep heavy cuts and fill away from the trees you're keeping. If an arena layout crowds a big cottonwood, we'll suggest shifting it.

Can you slope my paddocks so they drain into the ditch?

We won't send runoff into a ditch or onto its easement without the district's okay. Usually the better plan is to slope paddocks toward a spot on your own land where water can soak in.

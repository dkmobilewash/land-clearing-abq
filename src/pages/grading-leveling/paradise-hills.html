Leveling sand that won't sit still

The soil in Paradise Hills is mostly sand. It moves easily but won't hold a shape unless it's compacted, and wind across the mesa scours bare high spots and dumps the sand against walls.

We grade in compacted layers, slope the surface away from the house, and finish it ready for what goes on top, so the yard doesn't drift into a new shape by spring.

Finishes we often prep the ground for

  • Pavers or a patio slab
  • Planting beds with drip lines
  • A compacted base for a shed

Check your side of the zoning line before adding a structure

Paradise Hills is split. The unincorporated part falls under the Paradise Hills Special Zoning District, which has its own zoning commission rather than Bernalillo County's. Other addresses nearby are inside Albuquerque city limits, where city rules apply.

According to the county, new construction in the district needs a plot survey approved by the commission before you apply to the county for a building permit. If your grading is prep for an addition or another new structure, start that conversation early.

Paradise Hills grading questions

Does the special zoning commission need to sign off on a backyard regrade?

We can't answer for the commission. What the county does say is that new construction in the district needs a commission-approved plot survey before a county building permit. If your regrade is prep for a new structure, ask the commission early.

Will grading stop sand from drifting into my yard?

Grading alone won't stop the wind, but a compacted, sloped surface finished with gravel or another cover blows less than loose bare sand.

Can you grade a backyard that only has a side gate?

Often, yes. We bring compact equipment sized to the opening. Measure the gate width before you call and we'll know what fits.

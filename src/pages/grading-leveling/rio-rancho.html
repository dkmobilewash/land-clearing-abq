Grading jobs we see across Rio Rancho

The lot you've held for years

It was raw desert when you bought it and still is. After clearing, we rough grade the parcel so it looks usable to buyers and drains instead of collecting water in low pockets.

A pad ahead of your builder

Your builder or engineer gives us the pad elevation. We cut and fill to it, compact the fill in lifts, and leave the surrounding ground sloping away.

Backyard behind a newer home

Plenty of newer houses come with a backyard of raw, uneven fill. We level it, set the slope away from the slab, and prep it for gravel, pavers or a patio.

Sand drifted against walls

Wind piles sand on the upwind side of block walls and fences. We pull it back and regrade so the next storm doesn't push it straight back.

Sand that shifts and caliche that won't

Most of the mesa is fine, loose sand. It is easy to push, which sounds like good news, but a grade on it is only as good as its compaction. Loose fill settles, and bare slopes blow and wash once wind and monsoon rain get to them.

On some lots a hard caliche layer sits not far below the surface. When a cut reaches it, progress slows and we may need a ripper to get down to the elevation you need. If caliche looks likely, we check for it during the estimate.

Cleared sand starts drifting almost immediately, so we keep grading close behind clearing and wet the ground for dust control when the job calls for it.

What sets the size of a Rio Rancho grading job

We quote after seeing the lot. These are the details that move the scope up or down.

How much soil moves
A gentle lot needs a light pass. One with a dune or a dip may need fill brought in or extra sand hauled out.
Caliche in the cut
Hitting caliche adds ripping time and sometimes haul-off of broken material.
Road access
Parcels on unpaved roads at the edge of the city may need an access route or turnaround before trucks can deliver fill.
Compaction needs
A pad that will carry a house takes more careful compaction than a yard getting gravel.
Finding the corners
If survey pins are buried or missing, a surveyor may need to mark the lot before we grade near the lines.

Grading & leveling questions in Rio Rancho

How do you keep a graded lot from blowing away before I build?

We try not to leave graded ground bare longer than needed and can wet it while we work. For a lot that will sit a while, we can talk through leaving a rougher surface or adding erosion control.

My lot has caliche. Can you still reach the builder's elevation?

Usually, yes. We break through caliche with a ripper or, for harder spots, a hydraulic hammer. It adds time, so we'd rather find it during the estimate than halfway through the job.

Can you grade my lot if the survey pins are missing?

We can grade the interior, but near the property lines you should have a surveyor set or confirm the corners first. That keeps the work from straying onto a neighbor's lot.

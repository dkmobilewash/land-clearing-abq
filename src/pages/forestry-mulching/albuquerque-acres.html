Thinning brush instead of clearing the lot

Many owners in North Albuquerque Acres bought for the Sandia views and the open, native feel. Mulching fits that. We can thin dense brush, open a sight line from the house or cut a walking path, and leave the plants you want standing.

Because the mulch stays on the ground, the decomposed granite underneath doesn't sit bare. On a lot that slopes toward a wash, that cover helps slow runoff during a monsoon storm.

Good uses on these lots

  • Opening a view toward the mountains
  • Paths and trails across a large lot
  • Thinning dense brush near an existing home
  • Clearing a survey line or fence route
  • Knocking down growth before a builder walks the site

Mulch doesn't belong under a building pad

If you're building, the pad and driveway area need organic material removed so fill can be compacted properly. Mulching works well on the rest of the lot, but where the house goes we strip the mulch and roots and build the pad from the builder's or engineer's plan.

How owners on the big foothill lots use mulching

Built lot, overgrown edges

An established home with brush crowding back toward the house. We thin it back and leave a mulch layer instead of bare ground.

Vacant lot waiting for a build

Light mulching keeps the lot tidy and walkable for buyers or architects without disturbing the soil the way scraping does.

A lot with an arroyo

We mulch the upland part and stay out of the wash itself. Work in or near an arroyo may need approvals, so check with the city or county before planning anything closer.

Rock, slope and what they mean for mulching

Not every lot near the foothills suits a mulching head.

Surface rock
Mulching teeth that hit rock wear fast and can throw stones. Very rocky ground may be better cleared another way.
Slope
Tracked machines handle moderate slopes, but steep, loose ground limits where we can run.
Brush density
Thick, continuous brush takes longer than scattered shrubs, but it's also where mulching saves the most hauling.
Neighbors close by
Mulchers can throw chips, so we keep a margin near walls, cars and homes on the next lot.

Forestry mulching questions in Albuquerque Acres

Will mulching hurt the native plants I'm keeping?

Not if we mark them first and give them room. The machine works around flagged plants, and we leave a little extra space for shallow roots.

Do I need my HOA's approval to mulch?

Some subdivisions here have HOAs or architectural guidelines that may cover vegetation removal. Check with yours, and we are happy to talk the plan through with them.

Does the mulch break down quickly on these dry lots?

It breaks down slowly in a dry climate, so expect it to stay visible for a good while. If you want a cleaner look sooner, we can spread it thinner.

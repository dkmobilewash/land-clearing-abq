Mulching stops at the ditch easement

Many thickets in Corrales grow right along acequias and laterals, because that's where the water is. Those ditches and their easements are typically managed by the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District, and work in or next to them usually needs MRGCD's permission. Without it, we mulch to the edge of the easement and stop.

Why mulching suits tree rows, and where it falls short

The machine grinds invasive trees and brush where they stand and spreads the chips across the ground. You don't end up with a burn pile or a long string of haul loads leaving the property.

The tradeoff is the roots. Russian olive, Siberian elm and salt cedar all resprout from what is left in the ground. Mulching takes them down; keeping them down usually means grinding the larger stumps and dealing with new sprouts early.

Big cottonwoods are a different job. A mulcher handles brush and small to mid-size trees, so a large tree is cut and handled separately, and the ones you're keeping get a buffer that keeps the tracked machine off their roots.

What to sort out before mulching day

  • Which trees stay, flagged on the walk-through
  • Where the ditch easement starts on your side
  • Buried irrigation pipe, valves or turnouts in the work area
  • Whether you want the mulch left, spread thin or partly hauled
  • Recent irrigation, since soft ground near the bosque can delay heavy machines
  • Any project that may need a Village of Corrales permit or approval

Forestry mulching questions in Corrales

Can you mulch Russian olive with all those thorns?

Yes. Thorny Russian olive is a good fit for a mulcher, since nobody has to drag the branches to a pile. Bits of thorny wood can end up in the mulch, so think about where kids and animals walk.

How close can the mulcher get to my cottonwoods?

We agree on a buffer during the walk-through and keep the machine outside it. Brush inside that buffer is cut with smaller equipment or left alone.

Is the mulch good for my pasture?

A thin layer can shade and hold the soil, but a thick mat can smother grass you want back. Tell us how you will use the field and we will spread it, thin it or haul some away.

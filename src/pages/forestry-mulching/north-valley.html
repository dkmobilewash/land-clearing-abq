What decides how a North Valley mulching job goes

We look at these on every walk-through before we quote.

Stem size
Brush and small to mid-size elm and Russian olive mulch well. Old, large elms are cut and handled separately.
How wet the silt is
Tracked mulchers spread their weight, but valley clay after rain or irrigation can still rut. We schedule for firm ground.
Hidden obstacles
Old fence wire, metal posts and junk inside a thicket can damage a mulching head, so we look for them first.
Lot lines
Tree rows often sit on the line between two properties. We mulch your side unless both owners agree otherwise.
Ditch edges
If a ditch runs along the row, we stay out of the easement and keep chips out of the water.

Valley spots where a mulcher earns its keep

The back half of a deep lot

On long half-acre and acre lots, the back is often a thicket nobody has walked in years. Mulching opens it up so you can see what you actually have.

An abandoned orchard

Dead and dying fruit trees with brush grown up between the rows. We mulch the brush and smaller dead trees and leave any healthy ones you want.

Salt cedar in a soggy corner

Low spots that collect water are where salt cedar settles in. Mulching takes it to ground level.

Mulching on a lot with a well and septic

A mulcher works at the surface and doesn't dig, which makes it a gentler choice around the private lines many older valley properties have. Even so, a loaded tracked machine is heavy, and driving it over a septic tank or leach field is a bad idea.

Show us where your well, septic and any buried irrigation are during the walk. If stumps need grinding or pulling afterward, we request a New Mexico 811 locate first, knowing it won't mark private lines.

Whether your parcel is in the city, the county or the Village of Los Ranchos doesn't change how we mulch, but it does decide whose rules apply if you've received a weed notice.

Forestry mulching questions in North Valley

Will mulching get rid of my Siberian elm for good?

Not on its own. Elm comes back from roots and stumps, so mulching is usually the first step. Grinding the bigger stumps and catching new shoots early is what keeps a row from filling back in.

Can you mulch in winter when the trees are bare?

Often, yes, and bare branches make it easier to see what we are doing. The bigger question in the valley is whether the ground is firm enough, which depends on recent weather and irrigation more than the season.

Will the chips end up in my irrigation ditch?

We keep a margin between the mulching and the ditch so chips don't reach the water, and we clean up anything that lands near the edge on your side.

Buried rock under a finished yard

Close to the foothills, the rock usually isn't visible until you dig. A pool excavation or a retaining-wall cut can run cleanly for a few feet and then meet a boulder bigger than the bucket.

When that happens in an established yard, the challenge is less the rock than everything around it: pavers, irrigation, mature plants and a neighbor's wall a few feet away. We pick the tool for the space, which sometimes means a compact excavator with a small hammer rather than a large machine.

Putting your boulders back to work

Large rock dug out of these lots is often worth keeping. Set well, a boulder can anchor a planting bed, edge a path or step down a slope.

We can stage them during the dig and place them when the work is finished, following your landscape plan or your direction on site.

Rock & caliche questions in Tanoan

Can you move a boulder to a different spot in my yard instead of hauling it?

Yes, if there's a machine path to the new spot and the ground there can take it. We'll look at both locations during the estimate.

Will a hydraulic hammer bother my neighbors?

It's noisy while it runs. We limit it to approved hours, use it only as long as needed and try a ripper first when the rock allows.

Do I need HOA approval just to remove rock?

If the work changes how the yard looks or is part of a larger project like a pool, it typically needs architectural committee approval. Check with your HOA; we'll provide a written scope.

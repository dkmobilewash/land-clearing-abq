What caliche does to a backyard project

Caliche is soil cemented together with calcium carbonate, and in parts of Albuquerque it sits just under the surface. Water can't soak through it, so it pools above. Roots can't get through it either, which is why a tree in a small planting hole can sit for years without growing.

It also stops construction. A basement dig, a footing trench or a post hole can go from easy to impossible at the same depth. Once we break through, the rest of the work usually goes back to normal.

What drives the effort on an Albuquerque rock job

We quote after seeing the ground, because these change from yard to yard.

Thickness and hardness
A thin caliche crust rips easily; a thick, solid layer needs a hydraulic hammer and more time.
How deep you need to go
A planting bed is different from a basement or a footing.
Access
Older central neighborhoods often need a compact machine through a gate, which works slower than a full-size excavator.
Nearby structures
Breaking close to a house, wall or pool takes more care and sometimes a smaller tool.
Keep or haul
Boulders you want placed in the landscape cost time; rock you don't want costs haul-off.

Hammering near walls, pipes and neighbors

A hydraulic hammer is loud and transmits vibration, so we plan its use around block walls, foundations and your neighbors. Rock often hides utilities too, so we request a New Mexico 811 locate before breaking ground and dig carefully near the marks.

Rock & caliche questions in Albuquerque

Can you break caliche in a backyard I can only reach through a gate?

Usually. Compact excavators can carry a small hammer or ripper. It goes slower than a large machine, and we'll account for that in the quote.

Can I keep the boulders you dig out of my Foothills lot?

Yes. We can set them where you'd like them in the yard, or stack them for a landscaper. Anything you don't want is hauled off.

Will breaking caliche help a tree that keeps struggling?

It often helps. Breaking through the layer in and around the planting area lets water drain and roots spread. We can't promise how a particular tree will respond.

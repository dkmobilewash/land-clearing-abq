Mesa sand, with the occasional hard layer

The native ground on this part of the West Mesa is mostly sand. That's good news for digging, and it means a rock job here is usually about what's on top rather than what's below.

Some yards do have a cemented layer a little way down. If a post hole or footing in your yard stops short on something hard and chalky, it's worth a look before you assume the whole yard is like that.

Once old rock comes off, bare sand starts to blow. We plan the removal so the yard isn't left open longer than needed, especially if new landscaping is going in soon after.

Under the gravel: drip lines, fabric and cables

Old rock landscaping often covers weed-barrier fabric, drip tubing and sometimes low-voltage lighting wire. Tell us what you know is buried. For any digging beyond surface rock, we request a New Mexico 811 locate first.

What sets the size of a Paradise Hills rock job

Volume and access matter more here than hardness.

Depth of old gravel
Rock added over the years can be several layers deep, which multiplies the haul-off.
Boulder size
Small boulders load by hand or bucket; large ones need a machine path.
Access to the backyard
A narrow side gate means a compact machine and more trips.
Fabric and debris mixed in
Separating old fabric and drip line from the rock takes time.
Any hard layer below
If footings or planting need to go through cemented ground, breaking adds a step.

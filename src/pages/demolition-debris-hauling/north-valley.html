What's hiding behind an old valley property

Decades of farm and family use leave layers. A shed may have been patched with whatever was on hand, pens are often built from mixed wood and metal, and there's usually concrete from a structure that's no longer standing.

Older buildings may contain materials such as asbestos that need testing before they're disturbed. If something looks suspect during the job, we stop work on that part and tell you.

The ground underneath matters as well. River silt and clay go soft after rain or irrigation, so we haul when the yard can carry a loaded truck and keep heavy equipment off spots that are still wet.

Show us the wells, septic and buried pipe

Before breaking out slabs or digging footings, we request a New Mexico 811 locate. Private lines on older valley properties, such as well lines, septic systems and buried irrigation, aren't marked by 811, so point out anything you know about during the walk-through.

Demolition & hauling questions in North Valley

I got a notice about junk on my property. Who sent it?

The notice will say whether it came from the City of Albuquerque or Bernalillo County. Send it to us with the deadline and we'll plan the cleanup around it.

Can you tear down a shed that has power run to it?

Yes, once the power is disconnected. Have an electrician or the utility make it safe first; we don't start demolition on a structure with live service.

Do you take old tires and car batteries from the property?

They need special disposal, so they can't go in with general debris. Tell us how many there are at the estimate so the quote accounts for handling them separately.

Can you clear the junk without harming my old fruit trees?

Yes. Point out the trees and plantings you want kept during the walk-through. We work around them, load debris from the side away from their roots, and leave them as they were when the cleanup is done.

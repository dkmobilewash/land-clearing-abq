Village rules, utilities and old materials

The Village of Corrales has its own rules and permits, separate from Albuquerque and Rio Rancho. Taking down a structure can require a permit, so check with the village before we schedule.

Any power, water or gas to the building has to be disconnected first. Older barns and sheds may also contain materials such as asbestos that need testing; if we see signs of it, we stop and tell you before disturbing anything.

Sorted before demo day

  • Village permit confirmed, if one is needed
  • Electric, water and any gas disconnected
  • Horses moved away from the work area
  • Ditch and easement locations pointed out
  • Paint, chemicals, tires and batteries identified

What makes a Corrales demolition bigger or smaller

Every property here is different. These are the things we weigh when we write the quote.

Ground conditions
Land near the bosque or freshly irrigated fields can be too soft for loaded trucks, so timing matters.
Trees around the structure
A cottonwood beside a shed means slower, more careful work to keep debris and machines off its root zone.
Ditches nearby
Structures close to an acequia or lateral need extra care, and work in the ditch easement typically needs MRGCD permission.
Mixed materials
Wood, metal, concrete and roofing sorted into separate loads take more handling than a single pile.
Trailer access
Wide gates and long drives built for horse trailers help; tight gates add carrying time.

Nothing goes in the ditch

It's easy for scrap to slide into a nearby ditch during a tear-down. We stage piles away from acequias and laterals and never fill or block one. If a structure sits on a ditch easement, MRGCD's permission typically comes first.

Demolition & hauling questions in Corrales

Can you set aside lumber or metal panels I want to keep?

Tell us what you want before we start. We'll set aside pieces we can remove intact, though some material won't come out of a demolition in usable shape.

Will the trucks damage my pasture?

Soft ground is the main risk. We schedule hauling for when the ground is firm and choose a route across the property with you.

Do you remove old barbed wire and T-posts?

Yes. Old fencing of all kinds is part of the job. Wire and posts are pulled, bundled and hauled off with the other metal.

Where clearing starts on a Corrales property

A pasture gone to weeds and brush

Fields that haven't been grazed or irrigated in a few seasons fill in fast. We clear them back to open ground so you can reseed, irrigate or fence.

A tree row that's turned into a thicket

Old windbreaks along property lines are often mostly Russian olive, Siberian elm and salt cedar now. We take out the invasive growth and leave the trees you want.

A horse property being reset

Old pens, broken fencing and debris come out so you can lay out paddocks, an arena or a trailer turnaround.

The sandy west side

Higher lots climbing toward Rio Rancho are drier and sandier, with more open brush and fewer big trees. They clear faster but need a plan for blowing sand.

Clearing around trees the village wants to keep

Large cottonwoods are part of the character of Corrales, and many owners want every healthy one kept. We mark them during the walk-through and keep heavy equipment out from under them where we can, since compacted soil and torn roots hurt a tree you meant to save.

When an invasive tree is growing up against a cottonwood, we take it down in pieces instead of pushing it over. It's slower, but the tree you're keeping comes through intact.

The Village of Corrales has its own rules and permits. If you're planning a larger project, check with the village before we start.

Ask before anyone touches the ditch

Many lots here border an acequia or lateral run by the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District. Work in a ditch, on its banks or within its easement typically needs MRGCD's permission. We clear up to that line and leave the ditch alone unless you have approval.

Land clearing questions in Corrales

Can you clear my pasture without tearing up the irrigation?

Yes. Show us where the water comes in, where the borders and turnouts are, and any buried pipe. We work around them and leave the field ready to irrigate.

Do I need the village's permission to clear my land?

It depends on the project. Routine clearing on your own property is different from new structures or large grading work. Check with the Village of Corrales, and we can help you work out what to ask.

Will the Russian olive come back after clearing?

Russian olive and Siberian elm resprout from stumps and roots. Grinding or pulling the stumps cuts down on that, and new sprouts are easiest to handle while they are small.

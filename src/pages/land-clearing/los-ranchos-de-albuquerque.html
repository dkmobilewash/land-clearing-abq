Keeping land open in a village that values it

Los Ranchos de Albuquerque is its own municipality, with its own zoning and permits, and it puts real weight on open space and mature trees. That shapes how we clear here. The goal is rarely bare dirt. It's a usable field, a clean fence line or a lot you can see across again.

Before we take down any large tree, we suggest asking the village whether rules apply to your property. It's a quick question that can save trouble later.

Most of what we remove is the growth nobody planted on purpose: weeds, volunteer seedlings and the invasive trees that follow the water along ditches and fences.

Fields that went to weeds

An irrigated field that sits unused for a season or two fills with weeds and seedlings. Left longer, Russian olive and Siberian elm move in from the ditch banks and fence lines and start to take over.

Clearing early is easier and keeps the ground in shape for irrigation. We work the valley silt and loam when it's firm, so ruts don't spoil a field you plan to water again.

Getting a field back in use

  • Clear weeds, brush and young trees
  • Remove invasive trees along fence lines
  • Grind or pull stumps that would resprout
  • Haul off cuttings, or chip them and leave them
  • Follow with grading if water isn't spreading evenly

Ditches stay off-limits without approval

Most irrigation ditches here are managed by the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District. We won't clear in a ditch or on its banks without their approval, and we keep cut material and loose soil out of the water. Before stump work or any digging, we also request a New Mexico 811 locate.

Land clearing questions in Los Ranchos de Albuquerque

Can you clear around my orchard trees without damaging them?

Yes. We flag the trees you're keeping, keep heavy equipment out of their root zones where possible, and use smaller machines close to the trunks.

Should I check with the village before clearing my field?

Clearing weeds from your own field is usually routine upkeep, but larger projects, new structures and removing big trees are worth checking with the Village of Los Ranchos first. We can help you work out what to ask.

Will clearing change how my field takes irrigation water?

It can. Pulling out brush and roots often leaves high and low spots. If water isn't spreading evenly afterward, light grading can fix that, and we can quote it at the same time.

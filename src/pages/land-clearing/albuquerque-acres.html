Clearing a lot for a custom build

This is the usual order on an Albuquerque Acres site.

  1. 1

    Get the plan and staking

    We work from your builder's or engineer's site plan and staking, so we know where the pad, driveway and utility runs go.

  2. 2

    Flag what stays

    Native plants, boulders and trees you want to keep, along with the edges that frame your views, get marked before any machine moves.

  3. 3

    Clear the footprint

    We clear native brush from the build area, driveway line and trench routes, and remove roots wherever fill or a pad will go.

  4. 4

    Haul off or reuse

    Brush is hauled away, or mulched for disturbed ground outside the build area if you prefer.

Why two acre lots here can quote very differently

The same size lot can be a quick clear or a slow one.

How much of the lot you clear
Clearing only the build footprint is less work than clearing the whole parcel, and it keeps more of the native look.
Rock
Boulders and outcrops near the foothills may need a ripper or hydraulic hammer to move.
Slope
The land rises toward the Sandias, and steeper lots take more care to clear without starting erosion.
Arroyos and flood zones
A wash across the lot limits where machines can go and what can be disturbed.
Access
A paved street and a clear entry point make the job faster than a lot reached only by a dirt track.

Keeping the views and native plants you bought the lot for

Most owners here want their lot to feel like part of the foothills when the house is done. Scraping the whole parcel to dirt works against that, and it leaves a lot of bare decomposed granite to wash around in a monsoon storm.

So we clear tight to the plan. Trees and shrubs outside the work area stay, and we keep machine traffic on one route so the rest of the lot isn't trampled.

Before you clear, check

  • Whether your parcel falls under city or county rules
  • Any HOA or subdivision architectural guidelines
  • Whether part of the lot is in a mapped flood zone
  • If a fugitive dust control permit applies to the size of your project
  • Where your survey corners are

Arroyos change the clearing plan

If an arroyo crosses your lot, talk to your engineer and the city or county before clearing near it. Work near a wash may need extra approvals, and native growth on the banks helps hold them together. We clear to the approved plan and otherwise leave the channel alone.

Land clearing questions in Albuquerque Acres

Should I clear the whole acre or just the build area?

Usually just the build area plus the driveway and trench routes. It takes less work, keeps the native look and leaves less bare ground to erode. You can always clear more later.

Can you clear my lot before my builder is lined up?

We can, but clearing without a site plan risks removing plants you'd want to keep or opening up the wrong area. If the build is far off, a light cleanup of debris and dead brush is often the better first step.

Does the community association have a say in clearing?

There is a North Albuquerque Acres Community Association, and some subdivisions in the area have HOAs with architectural guidelines. Check what applies to your parcel before you clear.

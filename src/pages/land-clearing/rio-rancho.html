Raw mesa land, cleared all at once

Many of the parcels we clear in Rio Rancho were divided into lots decades ago and never touched. Out in Rio Rancho Estates, a typical lot is open sand with chamisa, tumbleweeds stacked against whatever stopped them, and sometimes a pile of dumped trash near the road.

Owners usually call when they're ready to list the lot or start a house, so the whole parcel gets cleared in one go. That's when the mesa wind matters: once the cover is gone, sandy ground starts to move.

Clearing here is usually light work on the vegetation and careful work on the planning. Getting the access, boundaries and timing right matters more than the brush itself.

What we check on a lot nobody has visited

  • Whether a truck and trailer can reach it on the existing road
  • Where the survey pins are, or whether they are missing
  • Caliche showing at the surface or in small washes
  • Dumped debris that needs separate disposal
  • Neighboring fences and walls catching tumbleweeds

Find your corners before the brush comes off

On older lots, survey pins are often buried in sand or gone entirely. Clearing the wrong strip is hard to undo, so if you can't point to your corners, have a surveyor mark them before we start. The City of Rio Rancho also sets its own rules, separate from Albuquerque's, so check with the city if you plan to build.

Land clearing questions in Rio Rancho

My lot is on an unpaved road. Can your equipment get there?

Most of the time. We check the road during the walk. If it's soft sand or badly rutted, we may suggest cutting a short access or turnaround first so trucks and later construction traffic can get in and out.

Will clearing make my lot blow away?

Bare sand does blow on the mesa. We avoid leaving more ground bare than your plan needs, and if you aren't building soon, partial clearing or leaving some cover can help.

Is there caliche under these lots?

Some have it, some don't. Clearing alone rarely reaches it, but if you're building, we'll tell you what we see so your pad and trenching plans account for it.

In the special zoning district, the plot survey comes first

If your home is in the unincorporated part of Paradise Hills, zoning is handled by the Paradise Hills Special Zoning Commission. Bernalillo County says new construction there needs a commission-approved plot survey before you apply to the county for a building permit, so footings for an addition usually wait on both. Addresses inside Albuquerque city limits go through the city instead.

Footings in sand that won't hold a straight wall

West Mesa sand comes out of the ground almost as fast as the bucket can swing. It also runs back in. A footing trench that sits open over a windy weekend can be half full by Monday, so we coordinate the dig with whoever is setting forms.

Wind matters for the spoil too. A pile of loose sand next to the block wall ends up in the neighbor's pool or on your patio, so we keep it low, wet it when needed and haul off what won't be reused.

Backfill gets the same attention. Sand compacts well in thin, moistened lifts and poorly when dumped back in, and a slab or patio laid over loose fill will tell you about it later.

Paradise Hills excavation questions

Does an addition here need anything before footings are dug?

If you're in the special zoning district, plan on a commission-approved plot survey and a county building permit first. Inside city limits, check with the City of Albuquerque. We can help you figure out which applies, but we can't decide it for you.

Why do footing trenches cave in so easily here?

The sand on the mesa has almost no binding to it. We dig close to when forms are ready, slope or support the sides as needed, and avoid leaving trenches open for long.

Can extra sand from my yard be hauled away?

Yes. Haul-off is included in the quote unless you'd rather keep some to raise a low spot or fill around the new foundation.

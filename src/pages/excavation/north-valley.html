Easy digging with a catch

The bucket moves through valley silt faster than almost anywhere in the metro. The trouble starts when the soil is wet: trench walls lose their shape, footing bottoms go soft and puddles sit in the excavation.

Backfill is the other half. Clay-heavy soil only compacts well near the right moisture, so we place it in thin lifts and won't bury a foundation in mud that will settle later.

What shapes a North Valley excavation quote

The soil is rarely the expensive part here. These are what move the effort up or down.

Timing around water
Irrigation days and storms can soak the site, and waiting for it to firm up affects the schedule.
Private lines
Wells, septic and old irrigation pipe have to be found by the owner or a private locator, then dug around carefully.
Reusing the spoil
Dry silt can often go back as backfill or fill a low spot. Saturated clay may need to be hauled and replaced.
Access past tree rows
Long rows of elm and Russian olive and narrow gates can limit which machine reaches the work.
Permit path
City and county parcels follow different processes, which can affect when work may start.

Find out whose rules apply to your parcel

Some North Valley addresses are inside Albuquerque city limits, others are unincorporated Bernalillo County, and the Village of Los Ranchos sits between them. Bernalillo County has an online tool that shows a property's permitting jurisdiction. Check it before you plan an addition or a large dig, then ask that office what the project needs.

Excavation questions in North Valley

Will New Mexico 811 mark my well line and septic?

No. 811 covers public utility lines. Private wells, septic and buried irrigation are the owner's to locate, so show us what you know and consider a private locator on older properties. We still request the 811 locate before every dig.

Can North Valley silt go back in as backfill?

Often, if it's at a workable moisture. Very wet or clay-heavy spoil may not compact well, and then we'd recommend hauling it and bringing in fill.

How long after irrigation can you excavate?

It depends on the soil and the weather. We check the ground the day before; if it won't hold a machine without rutting, we reschedule.

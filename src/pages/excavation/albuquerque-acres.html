Foundation excavation on a custom-home lot

This is the order we follow on most Albuquerque Acres builds, working from your builder's or engineer's plans and staking.

  1. 1

    Match the plans to the lot

    We walk the staking with the builder and note the pad elevation, rock outcrops and any drainage path crossing the site.

  2. 2

    Locate and clear the footprint

    After the New Mexico 811 marks are in, we clear only the building envelope and work area, not the whole parcel.

  3. 3

    Excavate to depth

    Footings, stem walls or a basement are dug to the plan. Where decomposed granite gives way to rock or caliche, we rip or hammer through it.

  4. 4

    Stockpile for reuse

    Usable spoil is set aside for the pad, backfill and final shaping instead of being hauled off.

  5. 5

    Backfill in lifts

    Once walls are up and inspected, we backfill and compact in lifts, shaping the ground so runoff moves around the house.

Why two neighboring Acres lots can quote differently

Lots that look alike from Paseo del Norte can be very different a few feet down.

How soon rock shows up
Some lots are decomposed granite to full depth; others hit boulders or caliche early in the dig.
Slope toward the mountains
Steeper lots mean deeper cuts on the uphill side and more fill on the downhill side.
Basement or slab
A basement multiplies the volume of material and the rock you're likely to meet.
What spoil can be reused
Clean decomposed granite often goes back into the pad. Broken rock may have to be hauled or sorted.
Driveway and access
A lot with no driveway yet may need a construction access cut before the excavator arrives.

Digging without losing the desert you bought

Most owners here chose their lot for the native vegetation and the Sandia views. A foundation dig doesn't have to scrape the whole acre to get done.

We keep the disturbance to the footprint and a working strip, put spoil piles where the builder needs them, and keep equipment off the areas you've flagged.

Arroyos and mapped flood zones

Some lots in North Albuquerque Acres have an arroyo crossing them or a portion mapped in a flood zone, and that can change where the house sits and how deep or high the foundation goes. Check your parcel's mapping and follow your engineer's design; grading near an arroyo may need additional approvals.

Excavation questions in Albuquerque Acres

Can you dig a basement on a rocky Albuquerque Acres lot?

Yes. We bring rippers and hydraulic hammers when the ground calls for them. A test dig during the estimate helps show how much rock to expect, so the quote is closer to what the excavation will actually involve.

Can the dirt from my foundation build up the pad?

Often. Clean decomposed granite usually makes good fill when compacted in lifts. Your engineer's specs decide what material is acceptable.

Who decides how deep the footings go?

Your engineer and builder, on the approved plans. We dig to those depths and the staking, not to our own judgment.

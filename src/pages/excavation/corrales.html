Digging between the ditch and the cottonwoods

Properties near the river sit on soil that has been irrigated for generations. It digs easily but stays soft, especially right after water has run through the fields. Climbing west toward Rio Rancho, the ground turns sandier and drier, with its own habit of caving.

The big cottonwoods are the other constraint. Their roots spread well past the canopy, so we keep tracks and spoil piles out of the zones you want protected and agree on those lines during the walk-through.

Most properties here are big enough to keep spoil on site. Many owners use it to fill a low corner of a pasture or build up a building site instead of paying to haul it away.

The ditch easement is not a staging area

Acequias and laterals here are managed by the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District. Digging in or next to a ditch or its easement typically needs their permission, and we won't park equipment, pile dirt or cut near one without it. The Village of Corrales has its own permits as well, so check with the village on structures and larger projects.

Questions from Corrales property owners

Can you dig a pond on a property near the bosque?

Often, yes, but the ground near the river can be soft and wet, and a pond near a ditch or easement may need MRGCD's permission. We look at the site and help you work out who to ask before we quote.

Will an excavator wreck my pasture or long driveway?

We pick the route in with you, wait for firm ground and keep heavy traffic to one path. If a section gets disturbed, we can grade it back before we leave.

Do I need village approval to excavate in Corrales?

It depends on the project. New structures and larger earthwork typically involve the Village of Corrales. Ask the village first; we can give you a written description of the work.

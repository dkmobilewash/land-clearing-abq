What we see after storms in Paradise Hills

Sand fanned across the patio

Water running off a roof or slab picks up sand and drops it on the first flat surface. A swale and a rock edge at the drip line usually stop it.

Back wall undermined

Runoff collecting along a block wall can wash out the sand beneath it. We regrade so water moves along and away from the wall instead of pooling at its base.

Yard cleared of weeds, then washed out

Pulling weeds leaves loose ground. Mulch, gravel or wattles on the cleared areas keep the next storm from carving it up.

Water heading toward a new patio or shed

We shape the grade around the new structure before it goes in, so runoff has a path past it.

Sand drains fast but doesn't hold its shape

Mesa sand absorbs a light shower easily, which is why many homeowners here don't notice a drainage problem until a heavy storm. When rain falls harder than the ground can take it, the surface starts moving with the water.

Wind makes it worse. Freshly regraded sand can blow out of a swale after a few dry, gusty weeks, so we treat surface cover as part of the drainage job, not an extra.

What affects drainage work in this neighborhood

Most jobs here are backyard corrections, but these things change the scope.

Your zoning authority
In the unincorporated part, the Paradise Hills Special Zoning District handles zoning; inside city limits, the City of Albuquerque does. New structures tied to the grading may need their sign-off.
Access to the backyard
Gate width decides whether we bring a compact machine or do more of the work by hand.
How much ground is bare
A fully cleared yard needs more surface cover than a single washout.
Walls and slabs
Block walls and patios limit where water can go and how we shape around them.
Neighbors downhill
Water can't be pushed onto adjoining lots, which sometimes means holding it in a small retention area on yours.
Buried lines
We request a New Mexico 811 locate before digging any swale or drain, and sprinkler lines you tell us about help avoid surprises.

Drainage & erosion questions in Paradise Hills

Does the Paradise Hills Special Zoning District need to approve drainage work?

We can't say what the commission will review on a given lot. What the county does say is that new construction there needs a commission-approved plot survey before a county building permit, so if the drainage work is part of an addition or new structure, check with the commission before we start.

Is a French drain useful in Paradise Hills sand?

Not often. Sand already lets water soak in, so the bigger risk is surface erosion. Grading, swales and rock usually do more here than a buried drain.

Can you keep my yard from blowing away after regrading?

We avoid leaving ground bare longer than necessary and cover finished areas with rock, gravel or mulch. On slopes, wattles hold things until permanent cover is in.

Typical drainage problems on village acreage

Arena that holds water for days

We regrade the arena base with a slight, even fall so rain moves off the riding surface toward a swale or ponding area at the edge.

Paddock churned into mud

Raising and shaping the area where horses gather, and steering barn roof runoff away from it, usually does more than any single drain.

Long driveway washing out on the west side

A crowned drive with rock-lined edges, or a culvert where runoff crosses, keeps sand from leaving with every storm.

Low spot near the house after irrigation

Gentle regrading toward a planned outlet, with care around the cottonwood roots that owners and the village want protected.

The ditch is not ours to change

If an irrigation ditch runs along your line, it is most likely an MRGCD acequia or lateral. Draining onto a ditch, filling part of its easement or reshaping a bank typically needs the district's permission, and we won't do it without that approval. We plan drainage that works on your side of the easement.

Drainage & erosion questions in Corrales

Do I need a village permit for drainage work in Corrales?

It depends on the size and type of work. The Village of Corrales has its own permits, so check with the village before larger grading jobs. Small yard corrections may not need one, but we'd rather you confirm.

Can grading hurt my cottonwoods?

It can if soil is cut away or piled over the root zone. We agree on which trees matter before we start and keep grade changes and heavy equipment out from under them as much as the job allows.

My neighbor's irrigation water floods my yard. Can you stop it?

We can usually reshape your side with a berm or swale so the water moves along the property line instead of into the yard. If it comes from a ditch, talk to the conservancy district about the ditch itself.

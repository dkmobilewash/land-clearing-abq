Flat ground is the real problem in the valley

Plenty of North Valley property was leveled long ago for flood irrigation, and level ground is exactly what you don't want around a house. Rain and irrigation water have nowhere to go, so they soak into silt that stays soft and sticky for days.

Clay-heavy spots are worse. Water sits on top, walkways and pens turn to mud, and the ground can take a long time to firm up. A small, consistent fall away from the house toward a planned spot often fixes more than any single drain.

Because this soil holds water instead of letting it drain straight down, French drains and gravel trenches earn their keep here more than they do on the sandy mesas.

What drives the work on a valley half-acre

These details change how much grading or drainage a property needs.

How flat the lot is now
Some yards need only a few inches of fall added; others were graded toward the house and need reshaping.
Where water can legally go
Without a street or allowed outlet to drain to, water may need to stay on site in a ponding area.
Old buried lines
Wells, septic systems and old irrigation pipe aren't marked by 811 and change where we can dig.
Soil moisture
Wet silt ruts under heavy machines, so timing may depend on weather and your irrigation schedule.
Horse pens and outbuildings
Each structure adds roof water and foot traffic that the grading has to account for.

Tell us these things before we dig

Older properties hide a lot underground. A few minutes on the phone helps us plan around it.

  • Where your well and septic tank or leach field are, if you have them.
  • Any buried irrigation pipe or valves, old or still in use.
  • Whether your address is in city limits or unincorporated county. Bernalillo County's online lookup will show you.
  • Which ditch serves the property and when you get water.
  • Where water stands the longest after a storm.

Ditches and easements stay as they are

Many lots here are served by Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District ditches. Drainage work can't block or fill a ditch or its easement, and any change to the ditch itself needs the district's approval. We also request a New Mexico 811 locate before excavating, though private lines on older valley lots won't show up on it.

Drainage & erosion questions in North Valley

Will a French drain clog in North Valley silt?

Fine silt can work into a drain over time, so we wrap the gravel in filter fabric and give the drain a clear outlet. Built that way, a French drain is one of the more useful tools on valley soil.

Who sets drainage rules for my lot, the city or the county?

It depends on which side of the line your parcel falls. Bernalillo County's online jurisdiction tool will tell you, and some nearby addresses are in the Village of Los Ranchos instead.

Can you fix a horse pen that stays muddy all winter?

Usually. We raise and compact the standing area, give it a slight fall, and move roof runoff away from it. In clay-heavy spots we may add a gravel base or a drain along the low side.

Where your water goes depends on which part of town you're in

On the Westside and Northwest Mesa, loose sand soaks up a light rain but erodes quickly under a hard monsoon burst. Water cuts small channels through bare yards and carries sand onto patios and sidewalks.

In the Northeast Heights and Foothills, decomposed granite and caliche shed water fast, and the slope adds speed. Runoff that starts uphill can reach a back wall or a foundation in seconds, so the fix is usually about slowing it down and steering it.

Down in the North Valley and South Valley, river silt does the opposite. It holds water and stays soft, and a yard that was graded flat can stay puddled for days after a storm.

Your neighbor's yard is not a drainage outlet

The city doesn't allow grading that sends stormwater onto neighboring property, and we design around that from the first sketch. Larger projects, or ones that change how water leaves the lot, may need an approved grading and drainage plan before work starts. We'll tell you if we think yours is headed that way.

Before we dig on your property

  • We request a New Mexico 811 locate so marked utilities are flagged before any trench or swale is cut.
  • Tell us about sprinkler lines, landscape lighting and anything else private you know is buried.
  • If the job covers a large area, a fugitive dust control permit from the city's air quality program may apply, and we help you check.
  • Point out downspouts, canales and wall weep holes, since they often explain where the water is coming from.

Drainage & erosion questions in Albuquerque

Can a French drain work in Albuquerque's sandy soil?

Sometimes, but sand takes water readily on its own, so a French drain isn't always the first tool. On the West Mesa we more often regrade and armor the surface. In valley silt or clay, a French drain makes more sense because the ground won't absorb water quickly.

Is it worth fixing drainage before monsoon season?

If you can, yes. Work finished before summer means the new grade and erosion control are settled for the first hard rain, instead of the ground being freshly disturbed when it arrives.

My lot is next to an arroyo. Can you shore up the bank?

Work in or along an arroyo usually needs approval from the city, county or flood control authority, depending on who manages it. We don't work inside an arroyo without those permissions, but we can grade and protect the part of your lot outside it.

Placing a pad on acreage near the river

On a large lot it's tempting to put the building wherever looks open. In Corrales, the open spot is often the lowest part of an old field, where irrigation water and monsoon runoff collect.

During the walk-through we look at where water sits, where the ditch runs, which trees are staying, and how a delivery truck or horse trailer will reach the building. That usually settles where the pad should go before your builder finalizes the plans.

Things we map on the walk

  • Cottonwoods and their root zones
  • Acequias, laterals and their easements
  • Low or wet areas in the field
  • Septic, well and buried irrigation you know of
  • Trailer and truck turning room

Ditches belong to someone else

Many Corrales properties border acequias and laterals managed by the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District. Grading in or next to a ditch or its easement typically needs MRGCD's permission, so a pad or driveway that crowds the ditch can hold up a project. We keep fill and equipment out of the easement unless you have that approval.

Soft field soil versus the sandy west side

Ground near the bosque and on freshly irrigated fields can be soft, and building a pad on top of soft, organic topsoil invites settling. We strip that layer from the footprint and replace it with fill compacted in lifts.

Climbing west toward Rio Rancho, the ground turns sandier and drier. It's easier to work after a storm but needs water to compact well and blows when left bare.

Either way, we schedule heavy equipment for when the ground is firm, so we don't rut the pasture getting to the pad.

Corrales site prep questions

Do I need a permit from the Village of Corrales before you build a pad?

Corrales has its own rules and permits, separate from Albuquerque and Rio Rancho, and new buildings usually go through the village. Check with them for your project; we can give you a written scope to include with your application.

Can you build a pad near a cottonwood without killing it?

We keep cuts, fill and equipment out of the root zone as far as the plan allows. If the pad has to sit close, we'll tell you during the walk-through so you can decide before the building is placed.

Can the barn pad and arena be done in the same job?

Yes. Doing them together lets us balance cut and fill across the site and grade both so water drains away from the barn.

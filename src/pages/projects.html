Projects we take on

Most calls fit one of these jobs. Here is where each one usually starts, what the work involves, and what you have when we leave.

Overgrown city lot after a weed notice

Where it starts: weeds, kochia and tumbleweeds past knee height, some trash, and a compliance date on the notice.

  • Weeds and brush cut low and raked up
  • Trash and dumped debris loaded out
  • Tumbleweeds pulled off walls and fences

You end up with: a clean lot, everything hauled away, and photos for your records if you need to show the city or county.

Brush & weed removal

Raw lot to a build-ready site

Where it starts: native brush, a few trees or stumps, uneven ground, and a builder waiting on the pad.

  • Clearing, stump removal and haul-off
  • Rough grading and a compacted building pad
  • Utility trenches after the 811 locate
  • Access cut in for trucks and equipment

You end up with: a site your builder can start on, graded so water moves away from the pad.

Site prep & building pads

Invasive trees taking over a yard or ditch bank

Where it starts: Siberian elm, salt cedar or Russian olive crowding a fence line, an acequia bank or a back pasture.

  • Trees felled and cut up
  • Stumps ground or pulled so they sprout less
  • Brush and wood hauled off, or left as mulch if you want it

You end up with: open ground and a plan for the re-sprouts these species are known for.

Tree & stump removal

Water running toward the house in monsoon season

Where it starts: puddling against the foundation, ruts across the yard, or a slope washing out after every storm.

  • Grade checked and reshaped to slope away from the house
  • Swales or rock rundowns where water needs a path
  • Care not to push water onto a neighbor’s property

You end up with: stormwater that leaves the yard where you want it to.

Drainage & erosion control

Rutted or washed-out dirt driveway

Where it starts: potholes, washboarding, or a low spot that turns to mud whenever it rains.

  • Driveway re-graded with a crown or cross-slope
  • Road base or gravel added and compacted
  • Culvert or drainage dip where water crosses

You end up with: a driveway that drains and holds up longer between storms.

Driveway grading & gravel

Old shed, slab or junk pile to remove

Where it starts: a falling-down shed, an old concrete pad, broken fencing, or years of piled-up materials.

  • Structure taken down and broken up
  • Concrete and debris loaded and hauled
  • Ground cleaned up and roughly leveled

You end up with: usable space and no pile left behind.

Demolition & debris hauling

What changes the size of a job

  • Lot size and how dense the growth is
  • Number and size of trees and stumps
  • Caliche or rock below the surface
  • Slope and room for equipment to get in
  • How much material has to be hauled away
  • How exact the finished grade needs to be
  • Permits, HOA approval or dust controls
  • Buried lines found during the 811 locate

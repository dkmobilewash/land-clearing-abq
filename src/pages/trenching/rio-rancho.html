Sand walls and long runs

Rio Rancho's sandy soil is quick to dig and quick to cave. A trench left open for a week in wind and dry weather can slump back in before the plumber shows up, so we line up the dig with your installers' schedule instead of digging early.

Some lots also have a layer of caliche below the sand. When the trench depth reaches it, progress slows and we may switch to a ripper or hammer. We look for signs of it during the estimate.

Lines we dig for on mesa lots

  • Water service from the meter to the house
  • Sewer connection, or the line out to a septic system where that's what the lot uses
  • Electrical service and conduit from the utility connection point
  • Trenches for a new gas line, dug for your licensed installer
  • Conduit to a detached garage, shop or well pump
  • Drip irrigation for new landscaping

Coordinating a Rio Rancho utility trench

  1. 1

    Confirm the route

    Your builder, plumber and electrician tell us where each line enters the lot and where it ends at the house, and the depth each one needs.

  2. 2

    Locate

    We request a New Mexico 811 locate and wait for marks before any digging, including on lots that look completely untouched.

  3. 3

    Dig to the installers' schedule

    Trenches open when the trades are ready to lay pipe and wire, which limits caving in the sand.

  4. 4

    Hold for inspection

    Many lines need to be inspected before backfill. We keep the trench open until the inspection has passed.

  5. 5

    Backfill and compact

    We backfill and compact the trench, so it doesn't settle under the driveway or the yard later.

Trenching questions in Rio Rancho

Can you trench before the house is framed?

Often, yes, and on many builds the water and sewer stub-outs are part of early site work. Your builder decides the sequence; we dig when it fits their schedule.

Does an empty desert lot really need an 811 locate?

Yes. Lines can run along roads and across parcels you'd never guess from the surface, and we request a locate before every dig.

Will sand from the trench blow all over the lot?

Spoil piles do dry out and drift on a windy day. Keeping the trench open only as long as needed is the main fix, and we can wet the piles when dust is a problem.

Backyard runs in older neighborhoods

Much of our city trenching is behind existing homes: power out to a detached garage, a sewer lateral for a casita, a new water service where the old pipe failed. These yards have walls, mature trees, patios and decades of lines that may or may not be on any drawing.

We bring compact machines that fit through the gate, keep spoil on tarps or in one spot so your yard isn't buried, and hand dig where the trench crosses marked utilities or tree roots you want to save.

Locate first, inspect before we fill

We request a New Mexico 811 locate before every trench, and we expose marked lines by hand where required. Many utility installations in Albuquerque need to be inspected before the trench is backfilled, so we leave it open until your plumber, electrician or gas installer tells us the line has passed.

Albuquerque trenching questions

Can you trench under a concrete walk or patio?

We can saw and break out a section, dig through, and leave it ready for your concrete contractor to patch. In some cases the installer can bore under instead; we'll talk through which makes sense for your yard.

How long does the trench stay open?

Until your installer has laid the line and any required inspection has passed. We schedule backfill with you so an open trench isn't left in the yard longer than it needs to be.

Will you put my yard back after trenching?

We backfill and compact the trench and rake out the area. Replacing sod, rock mulch or plants is usually up to you or your landscaper, and the quote spells out what's included.

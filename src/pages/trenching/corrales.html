Crossing a ditch is a separate conversation

If your line needs to cross or run next to an acequia or lateral managed by the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District, that work typically needs MRGCD's permission first. We route trenches to avoid the ditch and its easement where we can, and we won't dig in either without approval.

Routing around cottonwoods and soft ground

A straight line from the house to the barn is the cheapest trench on paper. In Corrales it often runs right through the root zone of a big cottonwood, and cutting those roots can harm the tree. We'll suggest a route that bends around trees you want to keep.

Fields near the bosque and freshly irrigated pasture can be too soft for the excavator. We plan the dig for when the ground is firm, and we keep the machine on one path so we don't rut the whole field getting to the trench.

Corrales trenching questions

Can you trench across my pasture without wrecking it?

We stay on one access path and backfill and compact the trench when the line is in. The strip over the trench will need time to grow back, and we can talk about reseeding it.

Can the water and power to my barn go in one trench?

Sometimes, depending on the code requirements and what your plumber and electrician want. They set any separation, and we dig to it.

My horses are on the property. How do you handle an open trench?

We'll talk through which pens to empty during the work, and we try to keep open sections short. Plan to keep animals away from the trench until it's backfilled.

Aging trees in a West Mesa neighborhood

Paradise Hills is an established neighborhood, and a lot of its trees have reached the end of their run. Some died back after dry years, some outgrew the yard, and many shrubs planted along the house now crowd walls and windows.

The sandy soil helps with stump work: grinders move through it easily and pulled stumps come out clean. The trade-off is that sand drifts once roots and ground cover are gone, so we talk with you about what will cover the spot afterward.

Wind affects the removal itself, too. On gusty mesa days we change how and when limbs come down so nothing catches a gust and swings toward a roof or fence.

Which rules apply
Inside the special zoning district, new construction goes through the commission. Inside city limits, City of Albuquerque rules apply.

Tree & stump removal questions in Paradise Hills

Does the zoning commission need to approve a tree removal?

For new construction, Bernalillo County says the commission must approve a plot survey before you apply for a building permit. Whether anything applies to removing a tree is a question for the commission, so ask them first if you're unsure.

Do you do tree work in Taylor Ranch and Ventana Ranch too?

Yes. We handle tree and stump removal across the Northwest Mesa, including Taylor Ranch, Ventana Ranch and Volcano Cliffs.

Can you leave the wood chips to cover the bare ground?

Yes. Chips from the limbs can be spread where the tree stood, which helps keep sandy soil from blowing. Tell us at the estimate and we'll leave them instead of hauling them off.

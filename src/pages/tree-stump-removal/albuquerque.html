Why the same stump takes different work across town

A stump on the Westside off Coors usually sits in loose sand, so the grinder cuts through cleanly and the hole is easy to backfill. In the Foothills and upper Northeast Heights, roots often wrap around buried boulders in decomposed granite, which slows grinding and can mean switching to an excavator.

In the North Valley and South Valley, river silt digs easily but stays soft after a storm or an irrigation turn. We look at the ground before deciding whether a heavier machine can work without leaving ruts.

Older neighborhoods near the university and Downtown add alleys, block walls and narrow side yards. Trees there usually come down in sections, lowered piece by piece instead of dropped whole.

Albuquerque tree jobs we're called for most

A neglected lot with a city notice

Overgrown lots often have volunteer elms along the fence line. If the City of Albuquerque has sent a weed and litter notice, we can take out the trees along with the weeds and send you before-and-after photos.

Clearing an infill lot for a new build

Builders need stumps and roots out of the footprint before the pad goes in. We pull them, backfill the holes and leave the lot ready for grading.

A dead tree over the patio

Drought and age leave plenty of dead trees standing over roofs and patios. We remove them in sections, chip the limbs and haul the wood unless you want to keep it for firewood.

An old stump that won't quit

Someone cut the tree years ago and left a stump that keeps throwing up shoots. Grinding it out or pulling it ends the cycle of cutting back sprouts every summer.

Trees near power lines are a utility job

If branches reach overhead lines, call your electric utility first. Line clearance belongs to qualified line-clearance crews, and we stay out of that zone. Once the utility has made it safe, we can usually take care of the rest of the tree and the stump.

Albuquerque tree and stump questions

Do I need a permit to remove a tree from my Albuquerque yard?

It depends on the tree and the property. A tree in the public right-of-way or one tied to a development approval may be treated differently from an ordinary backyard tree. If you're unsure, check with the City of Albuquerque; we can help you work out what to ask, but we don't give legal advice.

How do you get a stump grinder into a walled backyard?

We measure gate openings and check walls and overhead lines during the estimate, then bring a machine that fits. If nothing will fit, we tell you before you commit to anything.

What happens to the stump grindings?

Grindings are a mix of wood chips and soil. We can rake them into the hole, haul them away, or leave them for you to use as mulch.

Keep the brush out of the ditch

When a tree row runs along an MRGCD ditch, cut limbs and chips can easily end up in the water. We stack brush well back from the ditch and its easement and never block or fill it. Work in the ditch itself needs the district's approval.

Root systems in river silt

Valley silt and clay is soft compared with mesa sand or foothill rock, so a pulled stump usually comes out with a large, intact root ball. That clears the ground well, but it also leaves a bigger hole to backfill and compact.

The same soil holds water. After rain or an irrigation turn, a yard that looked firm can rut under an excavator, so we save stump pulling for dry spells and lean on grinding, which is lighter on the ground, when it's wet.

Elm rows here can run the full length of a property. Because Siberian elm re-sprouts from its roots, a row that is only cut to the ground tends to come back as a hedge of suckers.

Tree & stump removal questions in North Valley

Can you pull stumps near my septic tank or well?

Yes, with care. We request a New Mexico 811 locate before digging, but 811 doesn't mark private lines, so show us the tank, leach field, well and any buried irrigation. Stumps close to them are usually ground rather than pulled.

A tree row sits right on the line with my neighbor. Can you remove it?

We only remove trees you have the right to remove. Talk with your neighbor first, and if the boundary is unclear, a survey settles where the line is.

Why pull stumps in a horse pen instead of grinding them?

Grinding leaves roots and chips that settle as they rot, which can leave soft spots. If the pen is going to be regraded, pulling gives you a firmer base.

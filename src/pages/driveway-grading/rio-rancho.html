Sand makes a soft driveway

Loose mesa sand is the main challenge for a Rio Rancho driveway. Drive on it unprepared and tires dig in, ruts form, and a loaded concrete or lumber truck can get stuck. A drive here needs a compacted subgrade and real base course, not just gravel spread over sand.

At the edges of the city, where roads are still dirt, reaching the lot can be part of the problem. We check how a truck and trailer get to your parcel and whether a turnaround is needed so delivery drivers aren't backing out a long way.

Cutting in the drive before construction keeps the rest of the lot from being torn up by every truck that picks its own path.

Building a new driveway on a vacant Rio Rancho lot

  1. 1

    Confirm the lines

    On older lots, survey pins can be buried or missing. If the corners aren't clear, have a surveyor mark them so the drive stays on your parcel.

  2. 2

    Clear and cut in

    We clear chamisa and brush along the driveway path, then cut the subgrade with a crown or cross-slope so water sheds off.

  3. 3

    Base course in lifts

    Base course goes down and is compacted, building a firm layer that won't push into the sand.

  4. 4

    Surface gravel and turnaround

    Crushed surface gravel goes on top, along with any turnaround or parking pad you need for construction or later.

Driveways questions in Rio Rancho

Can you build a driveway on a lot along an unpaved road?

Yes. We look at the road itself during the estimate, since the truck hauling base course has to get there too. If the last stretch is soft sand, we'll explain what that means for the job.

Why not just dump gravel on the sand?

Gravel spread on loose sand sinks and scatters, and ruts come right back. Compacted base course underneath gives the surface gravel something to sit on.

Should the driveway go in before or after my house?

Many owners cut in an access drive first so construction traffic stays on one path, then have it reworked or resurfaced once the heavy trucks are done.

Keeping runoff off a sloped driveway

Land in the Acres rises toward the mountains, so storm water travels downhill across most lots. A drive laid straight down the slope becomes a channel in a monsoon storm and carries its gravel to the street.

We shape drives with a cross-slope or crown, add drainage dips that turn water off at intervals, and place culverts where a drainage path crosses. The goal is to move water off the drive and around the house without dumping it on a neighbor.

Fixing an older drive in the Acres

Established homes here often have gravel drives that wash out a little more each summer, leaving a rut down the middle and a pile of gravel at the bottom. Usually the drive has lost its shape and its base, and water has found the easy path straight down it.

We pull the gravel back, rebuild the base where it's gone, restore the crown and add drainage dips. On steeper stretches, more turnouts keep the water from picking up speed.

Driveways questions in Albuquerque Acres

Do I need approval before my new drive meets the street?

Where a driveway joins a public road, you may need approval from the road authority, and which one depends on whether your parcel is city or county. Some subdivisions here also have architectural guidelines that cover drives. Check before we cut in.

Can my driveway cross the arroyo on my lot with a culvert?

Possibly, but grading near an arroyo may need extra approvals, and some lots fall in a mapped flood zone. Sort that out with the right agency first, and we'll build to the approved plan.

Why does my gravel keep ending up at the bottom of the drive?

Water is running down the drive instead of off it. Recrowning and adding drainage dips to turn the water aside usually helps the gravel stay put.

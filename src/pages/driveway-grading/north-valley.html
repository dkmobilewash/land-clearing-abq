Old farm lanes on river soil

Lots of North Valley driveways started as farm lanes: a strip of packed dirt along a tree row or ditch that picked up some gravel over the years. On river silt and clay, that rarely holds. The soil softens every time it gets wet and works up through the gravel.

Doing it properly means lifting the drive slightly above the ground around it and building base underneath. On half-acre and acre lots that can be a long run, often past a horse pen, well or septic area we have to work around.

Point these out before we work on your drive

  • Well lines, septic tanks or leach fields near the driveway
  • Buried irrigation pipe crossing under it
  • Where the drive meets a ditch or its easement
  • Whether your parcel is in city limits, the county, or near the Los Ranchos line
  • Where you park trailers, RVs or equipment

811 won't mark private valley lines

We request a New Mexico 811 locate before we dig, but that covers utility-owned lines. Private wells, septic systems and old buried irrigation on older valley properties often go unmarked, so we count on you to show us what you know is there.

Driveways questions in North Valley

Can you add a culvert where my driveway crosses the irrigation ditch?

Work in a ditch or its easement needs the Middle Rio Grande Conservancy District's approval first. Once you have it, we can set the culvert and rebuild the drive over it.

Who do I ask about a new driveway connection to the street?

It depends on whether your parcel is inside Albuquerque city limits or in unincorporated Bernalillo County. The county's online jurisdiction tool can tell you, and then you'd check with that road authority.

Will crowning my driveway send water onto my neighbor?

We shape the crown so water sheds to your side of the line and spreads out or reaches a spot where it can soak in, rather than running straight onto the next lot.
